# Chapter 1 — The Knowledge Problem

Humanity has created more information than at any point in history. Yet that information does not form a shared body of knowledge.

Facts, research, observations, and expertise are scattered across websites, academic publications, databases, government repositories, enterprise systems, and increasingly, AI platforms. Each system maintains its own representation of the world, with limited interoperability between them.

## Knowledge Is Fragmented

The same entity may exist independently in thousands of databases, represented differently in each. Relationships between information are often trapped within organizational or platform boundaries.

As a result, applications repeatedly reconstruct knowledge that already exists elsewhere.

Search engines index documents. Databases store records. Knowledge graphs connect entities. AI models encode patterns learned from enormous datasets.

But these systems largely remain isolated from one another.

There is no common knowledge layer for the internet.

## Knowledge Is Controlled

Much of the world's structured knowledge is maintained by organizations that control its storage, access, governance, and availability.

Even when information is publicly accessible, the infrastructure organizing it may not be.

This creates dependency on individual platforms. A provider can restrict access, change APIs, modify policies, alter ranking mechanisms, or discontinue a service entirely.

A global knowledge infrastructure should not depend on the continued existence or neutrality of any single organization.

## Knowledge Loses Its Provenance

Information without provenance is difficult to trust.

When a claim moves through articles, databases, social networks, search engines, and AI systems, its connection to the original evidence can become increasingly weak.

Users are often presented with an answer without being able to easily determine:

- who made the underlying claim,
- what evidence supports it,
- when it was established,
- whether it has been challenged,
- or whether newer evidence has superseded it.

This problem becomes more significant as AI systems increasingly mediate access to information.

## AI Makes the Problem More Urgent

AI is changing how knowledge is consumed.

Instead of searching for documents and evaluating them individually, people increasingly ask AI systems questions and receive synthesized answers.

But AI systems need reliable knowledge to reason over.

Without an open knowledge infrastructure, AI applications must depend on proprietary datasets, independently constructed knowledge graphs, search indexes, or information embedded within model parameters.

Every organization therefore builds another partial representation of the same world.

## Knowledge Should Be Shared Infrastructure

The internet created open protocols for exchanging information, but it never created an equivalent protocol for maintaining interconnected knowledge.

Such infrastructure should allow knowledge to be created by many participants, connected across domains, traced back to its sources, challenged when necessary, and updated as understanding evolves.

Most importantly, it should not require a single organization to own the resulting graph.

**Knowledge is a shared human resource. The infrastructure that connects it should be shared as well.**
