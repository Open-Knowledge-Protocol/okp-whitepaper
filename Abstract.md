# Abstract

The internet made information globally accessible, but knowledge remains fragmented across websites, databases, research repositories, organizations, and AI systems. There is no shared infrastructure where knowledge can be connected, traced to its sources, challenged, verified, and continuously improved without being controlled by a single organization.

**Open Knowledge Protocol (OKP)** proposes an open protocol for building a decentralized, global knowledge graph.

OKP represents knowledge as interconnected **entities, claims, relationships, sources, evidence, and provenance**. It does not attempt to define a single version of truth. Conflicting claims can coexist, while evidence, verification, reputation, and provenance allow people and applications to decide what they trust.

At the infrastructure level, OKP introduces the **Open Graph Chain** — a decentralized, graph-native network where the knowledge graph itself is the network state. Independent nodes collectively store, replicate, validate, index, and serve the graph.

The system is organized around four layers:

**Application Layer → Knowledge Layer → Trust Layer → Open Graph Chain**

Governance is separated from truth. The **OKP DAO** governs the protocol, network rules, and shared resources, but it does not decide which claims are true.

The long-term vision is a shared knowledge infrastructure that humans, organizations, applications, and AI agents can contribute to and build upon without requiring permission from a central owner.

**Knowledge should be connected.  
Evidence should be traceable.  
Disagreement should be preserved.  
And the infrastructure holding humanity's shared knowledge should belong to no single organization.**
