# Chapter 17 — DAO Governance

Open Knowledge Protocol is intended to become public infrastructure.

For that to happen, control of the protocol cannot remain permanently with its original creators or any single organization.

OKP will therefore be governed through a **Decentralized Autonomous Organization (DAO)**.

## What the DAO Governs

The DAO governs the rules and evolution of the protocol.

This may include:

- Protocol upgrades
- Knowledge schema standards
- Network parameters
- Node requirements
- Economic parameters
- Treasury management
- Ecosystem grants
- Security processes

For example:

```text id="spvxh8"
Community
    │
    ▼
Proposal
    │
    ▼
Discussion
    │
    ▼
Vote
    │
    ▼
Protocol Change
```

Changes are proposed openly and become part of a transparent governance process.

## What the DAO Does Not Govern

The DAO does **not** decide what is true.

A governance vote should never be able to declare:

**“Claim A is true.”**

or:

**“Claim B should be removed because the majority disagrees with it.”**

Knowledge is evaluated through evidence, provenance, verification, reputation, and application-specific trust models.

This separation protects OKP from becoming a centralized authority under a decentralized name.

**The DAO governs the protocol. It does not govern truth.**

## Protocol Changes

Anyone meeting the governance requirements should be able to propose improvements.

A proposal might introduce:

```text id="a1agq1"
New Relationship Type
New Graph Operation
New Query Capability
Change in Replication Rules
Change in Network Fees
Protocol Security Upgrade
```

Major changes should go through public discussion and review before activation.

This gives applications and node operators time to understand and prepare for changes.

## Delegated Governance

Not every participant will have the expertise or time to evaluate every proposal.

Participants may therefore delegate voting power to people or groups they trust.

Different working groups may also develop expertise in areas such as:

```text id="pvgfz6"
Protocol Engineering
Graph Architecture
Security
Knowledge Standards
Economics
AI
```

These groups can research and recommend changes without gaining permanent control over the protocol.

## Preventing Governance Capture

Decentralized governance can still become centralized if a small number of participants control most decisions.

OKP governance should therefore be designed to reduce risks such as:

- Concentration of voting power
- Low voter participation
- Coordinated attacks
- Conflicts of interest
- Permanent control by early participants
- Governance through economic wealth alone

The exact governance mechanism can evolve as the network matures.

Decentralization should be measured by how difficult it is for any participant or group to control the protocol—not simply by the existence of voting.

## Treasury

The DAO may maintain a treasury used to support the ecosystem.

Funds could support:

- Protocol development
- Security audits
- Research
- Public datasets
- Developer tools
- Node infrastructure
- Community projects
- Ecosystem grants

Treasury spending should be transparent and governed through the same open process.

## Emergency Governance

Some situations, such as a critical security vulnerability, may require faster action than normal governance allows.

OKP may therefore support limited emergency mechanisms.

Such powers should be narrowly defined, transparent, time-limited, and removable by the DAO.

Emergency governance should protect the network without becoming a permanent backdoor for centralized control.

## Progressive Decentralization

OKP may begin with a smaller group responsible for building and securing the initial protocol.

Control should progressively move toward the DAO as the network becomes stable.

```text id="hdgkkr"
Core Contributors
       │
       ▼
Community Governance
       │
       ▼
      DAO
       │
       ▼
Permissionless Protocol
```

The objective is not decentralization for appearance.

The objective is to ensure that the Open Knowledge Graph can continue to exist and evolve even if its original creators disappear.

**Organizations may contribute to OKP. Communities may improve it. Applications may build businesses on it. But no organization should own the protocol or the knowledge graph it creates.**
